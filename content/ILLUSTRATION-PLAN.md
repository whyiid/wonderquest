# Which articles need an illustration

Decided 2026-09-13. The rule: **a picture earns its place when it carries
something the words cannot** — an animal nobody has seen, a structure that is
hidden, a place, an object. It is dead weight when the subject is abstract, or
when the picture would only be decoration.

Articles with no image are not broken. They show their emoji on the category
gradient, which looks deliberate rather than missing.

12 of 91 articles already have artwork. Of the remaining 79: **27 to do, 27
worth doing, 25 to leave alone.**

## Tier 2 — worth doing after Tier 1 (27, three collages)

A diagram makes the mechanism click faster than a paragraph does.

**Mechanisms** — how-a-wing-lifts, how-a-fridge-makes-cold, how-soap-works,
how-solar-panels-work, how-speakers-make-sound, battery-stores-chemistry,
touchscreen-feels-electricity, how-a-microwave-works, how-gps-knows-where-you-are

**Earth and sky** — why-the-moon-has-phases, why-we-have-seasons,
where-rain-comes-from, why-the-sky-is-blue, shooting-stars-are-tiny,
how-mountains-grow, why-planes-leave-white-trails, cats-land-on-their-feet,
continents-that-move

**Work, not faces** — ibn-al-haytham, marie-curie, ada-lovelace, alan-turing,
katherine-johnson, jane-goodall, srinivasa-ramanujan, x-rays-discovered,
penicillin-by-accident

> **People are a special case.** Do not generate AI portraits of real people.
> An invented face of Marie Curie is a fabricated likeness of someone who
> actually existed, in an app whose whole purpose is learning true things — and
> real photographs of Turing, Johnson and Goodall are still in copyright, which
> matters because this app is published. Illustrate what they *did* instead:
> Curie's glowing vial in a dark shed, Lovelace's punched cards and gears,
> Turing's code-breaking machine, Johnson's trajectory on a blackboard,
> Goodall's chimpanzee stripping a twig, Ramanujan's notebook page,
> Ibn al-Haytham's dark room with an upside-down image on the wall.

## Tier 3 — no image, on purpose (25)

Generating these would waste effort and add nothing.

**Abstract — nothing to draw**: what-is-consciousness, why-is-there-anything,
why-do-we-sleep, how-did-life-begin, dark-matter, unexplored-deep-ocean,
why-computers-use-ones-and-zeros

**Already familiar** — he knows what these look like: flamingos-are-pink-from-food,
birds-sleep-while-flying, ants-count-their-steps, bats-see-with-sound,
arctic-tern-longest-journey, whale-songs-change, honey-never-spoils

**Body topics where a picture adds nothing a child needs**: why-you-blink,
why-you-get-goosebumps, how-cuts-stop-bleeding, what-a-fever-is-for,
tongue-map-is-wrong, why-you-have-fingerprints, microbes-living-in-you

**Historical scenes better told than drawn**: germs-cause-disease,
first-vaccine, how-old-is-the-earth

If one of these later turns out to be a favourite of his, it can always get an
illustration then. The reverse — generating 79 images and finding half unused —
is the waste worth avoiding.
